    India

    The India route: 3 regions, 12 landmarks. Every landmark is built in three stages.

    Delhi

    Route steps 1–12

    • Red Fort

      The Red Fort takes its name from its red sandstone walls. It became a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2007. Source: Wikipedia

    • Qutub Minar

      At 72.5 metres, the Qutub Minar is the tallest minaret in the world built of bricks. Source: Wikipedia

    • Humayun's Tomb

      Humayun's Tomb was the first garden-tomb on the Indian subcontinent. UNESCO listed it in 1993. Source: Wikipedia

    • Lotus Temple

      The Lotus Temple is made of 27 marble-clad petals, set in three rings of nine like a half-open flower. Source: Wikipedia

    Agra

    Route steps 13–24

    • Taj Mahal

      Emperor Shah Jahan ordered the Taj Mahal in 1631 to hold the tomb of his late wife, Mumtaz Mahal. Source: Wikipedia

    • Agra Fort

      Emperor Akbar rebuilt Agra Fort in red sandstone between 1565 and 1573. Source: Wikipedia

    • Buland Darwaza, Fatehpur Sikri

      Emperor Akbar began this giant gateway in 1573 to celebrate a victory. It rises about 54 metres from the ground. Source: Wikipedia

    • Mehtab Bagh Garden

      Mehtab Bagh means ‘Moonlight Garden’. It sits across the river, perfectly lined up with the Taj Mahal. Source: Wikipedia

    Jaipur

    Route steps 25–36

    • Hawa Mahal

      Hawa Mahal, the ‘Palace of Winds’, has 953 small windows arranged like a honeycomb. Source: Wikipedia

    • Amber Fort

      Inside Amber Fort is the Sheesh Mahal, a mirror palace whose tiny mirrors sparkled in candlelight. Source: Wikipedia

    • Jal Mahal Water Palace

      Jal Mahal has five floors, but when Man Sagar Lake is full, its lowest floor stays underwater. Source: Wikipedia

    • Jantar Mantar Observatory

      Jantar Mantar, finished in 1734, has the world's largest stone sundial. Source: Wikipedia
