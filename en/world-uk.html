    United Kingdom

    The United Kingdom route: 3 regions, 12 landmarks. Every landmark is built in three stages.

    London

    Route steps 1–12

    • Big Ben Clock Tower

      Big Ben is really the name of the great bell inside, which weighs about 13.7 tonnes. The tower was finished in 1859. Source: Wikipedia

    • Tower Bridge

      Opened in 1894, the bridge's middle splits in two and lifts up so tall ships can sail through. Source: Wikipedia

    • Tower of London

      The White Tower, which gives the castle its name, was built in 1078. Today the Crown Jewels are kept here. Source: Wikipedia

    • Giant Wheel on the Thames

      This giant wheel on the Thames is 135 metres tall. When it opened in 2000, it was the tallest Ferris wheel in the world. Source: Wikipedia

    Windsor · Oxford · Cambridge

    Route steps 13–24

    • Windsor Castle

      Kings and queens have used Windsor Castle since the 1100s, making it the longest-occupied palace in Europe. Source: Wikipedia

    • Radcliffe Camera

      This round building was built as a library between 1737 and 1749. Today Oxford students use it as a reading room. Source: Wikipedia

    • Punting in Cambridge

      A punt is a flat-bottomed boat. Instead of rowing, you push a long pole against the riverbed; in Cambridge punts carry visitors. Source: Wikipedia

    • Kew Gardens Glasshouse

      Kew's Palm House was built between 1844 and 1848. It was the first large-scale use of wrought iron to hold up a building. Source: Wikipedia

    Stonehenge · Bath

    Route steps 25–36

    • Stonehenge

      The great stone circle was set up between 2600 and 2400 BC. It lines up with the sunrise on the summer solstice. Source: Wikipedia

    • Roman Baths of Bath

      Every day, about 1.17 million litres of hot water at 46 °C bubble up from the spring here. Source: Wikipedia

    • Pulteney Bridge

      Finished by 1774, it is one of only four bridges in the world with shops along its whole length on both sides. Source: Wikipedia

    • Cotswolds Stone Villages

      The village houses are built from golden local Cotswold limestone. In the Middle Ages, the area grew rich from wool. Source: Wikipedia
