    Brazil

    The Brazil route: 3 regions, 12 landmarks. Every landmark is built in three stages.

    Amazon

    Route steps 1–12

    • Manaus Opera House

      This opera house in the middle of the Amazon rainforest opened on 31 December 1896. Source: Wikipedia

    • Amazon Riverboat

      The Amazon carries more water than any other river on Earth, yet no bridge crosses its whole width. Source: Wikipedia

    • Meeting of the Waters

      The dark Rio Negro and the pale Amazon flow side by side for 6 km without mixing. Source: Wikipedia

    • Rainforest Treehouse

      The Amazon rainforest is thought to have about 390 billion trees of around 16,000 different kinds. Source: Wikipedia

    Lençóis · Northeast

    Route steps 13–24

    • Lençóis Dunes and Lagoons

      In the rainy season, the hollows between the sand dunes fill with freshwater lagoons. It became a UNESCO site in 2024. Source: Wikipedia

    • Pedra Furada, Jericoacoara

      Pedra Furada is a huge rock arch, shaped like a doorway by the ocean waves. Source: Wikipedia

    • Fernando de Noronha Islands

      These islands became a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2001 and are home to many spinner dolphins. Source: Wikipedia

    • Colourful Houses of Olinda

      Founded in 1535, Olinda's colourful old centre was named a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1982. Source: Wikipedia

    Salvador · Bahia

    Route steps 25–36

    • Colourful Pelourinho

      Salvador's colourful old centre, Pelourinho, became a UNESCO site in 1985 and is a heart of Afro-Brazilian music. Source: Wikipedia

    • Lacerda Elevator

      Finished in 1873, this 72-metre elevator links the lower and upper city in a 30-second ride. Source: Wikipedia

    • Barra Lighthouse

      Today's lighthouse was built in 1839; its 22-metre tower is painted with black and white stripes. Source: Wikipedia

    • Morro de São Paulo Beach

      Morro de São Paulo's beaches are named with numbers: First, Second, Third, Fourth and Fifth Beach. Source: Wikipedia
