    Türkiye

    The Türkiye route: 14 regions, 56 landmarks. Every landmark is built in three stages.

    Istanbul · Historic Peninsula

    Route steps 1–12

    • Hagia Sophia

      Completed in 537, Hagia Sophia remained the world's largest church for more than 500 years. Source: Wikipedia

    • Blue Mosque

      The Blue Mosque has six minarets. Inside, thousands of mostly blue Iznik tiles decorate its walls. Source: Wikipedia

    • Grand Bazaar

      The Grand Bazaar has 61 covered streets and more than 4,000 shops. Source: Wikipedia

    • Basilica Cistern

      The ceiling of the Basilica Cistern rests on 336 marble columns, each 9 metres tall. Source: Wikipedia

    Istanbul · Bosphorus

    Route steps 13–24

    • Galata Tower

      Galata Tower was built in 1348. At 66.9 metres, it was then the tallest building in the city. Source: Wikipedia

    • Maiden's Tower

      Maiden's Tower sits on a tiny islet 200 metres off Üsküdar. It appeared on the Turkish 10 lira banknote from 1966 to 1981. Source: Wikipedia

    • Bosphorus Bridge

      Opened in 1973, the Bosphorus Bridge links Europe and Asia. It is 1,560 metres long. Source: Wikipedia

    • Ortaköy Mosque

      Ortaköy Mosque was completed around 1854–1856. Its architects, the Balyan father and son, also designed Dolmabahçe Palace. Source: Wikipedia

    Edirne

    Route steps 25–36

    • Selimiye Mosque

      Mimar Sinan built Selimiye Mosque in 1568–1575 and called it his masterpiece. It became a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2011. Source: Wikipedia

    • Üç Şerefeli Mosque

      Üç Şerefeli Mosque was built in 1438–1447. Its name comes from its minaret with three balconies. Source: Wikipedia

    • Meriç Bridge

      Meriç Bridge was completed in 1843. The 263-metre bridge has twelve arches. Source: Wikipedia

    • Kırkpınar Wrestling Arena

      Kırkpınar oil wrestling holds the Guinness World Record for the longest-running athletic competition. Source: Wikipedia

    Çanakkale

    Route steps 37–48

    • Trojan Horse

      At the entrance to the ancient city of Troy near Çanakkale stands a giant wooden horse monument you can climb up a ladder and explore inside. It remembers the famous legend of the Trojan Horse, the clever trick Greek soldiers used to sneak into the city. Source: Wikipedia

    • Walls of Troy

      Archaeologists found nine city layers built on top of each other at Troy. It joined the UNESCO World Heritage list in 1998. Source: Wikipedia

    • Çanakkale Clock Tower

      Çanakkale Clock Tower was built in 1896. The five-storey tower is about 20 metres tall. Source: Wikipedia

    • Kilitbahir Castle

      Kilitbahir Castle was built by Sultan Mehmed II in 1463. Its name means ‘lock of the sea’. Source: Wikipedia

    İzmir · Ephesus

    Route steps 49–60

    • Library of Celsus

      The Library of Celsus is believed to have held about 12,000 scrolls. It was the third-largest library of the ancient Greco-Roman world. Source: Wikipedia

    • Temple of Artemis Wonder

      The Temple of Artemis at Ephesus is one of the Seven Wonders of the Ancient World. Today only its foundations and pieces remain. Source: Wikipedia

    • Konak Clock Tower

      Konak Clock Tower opened in 1901. The 25-metre tower has four fountains around its base and is İzmir's symbol. Source: Wikipedia

    • Historic Elevator

      İzmir's historic Asansör was built in 1907. It carried people up the steep cliff in Karataş, from the shore to the hilltop. Source: Wikipedia

    Pamukkale

    Route steps 61–72

    • Travertine Terraces

      Pamukkale means ‘cotton castle’. Its snow-white travertine terraces are made by minerals from hot springs. Source: Wikipedia

    • Hierapolis Theatre

      The ancient theatre of Hierapolis could seat about 15,000 people. Hierapolis became a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1988. Source: Wikipedia

    • Cleopatra's Pool

      In Cleopatra's Pool you can swim above ancient marble columns lying under the water. Source: Wikipedia

    • Tetrapylon of Aphrodisias

      The Tetrapylon of Aphrodisias was built around AD 200. In 1991 it was raised again using about 85% of its original stones. Source: Wikipedia

    Bodrum · Fethiye

    Route steps 73–84

    • Mausoleum at Halicarnassus Wonder

      The Mausoleum at Halicarnassus was one of the Seven Wonders of the Ancient World. The word ‘mausoleum’ comes from its owner, Mausolus. Source: Wikipedia

    • Bodrum Castle

      Bodrum Castle was built from 1402 onwards. Today it is home to the Museum of Underwater Archaeology. Source: Wikipedia

    • Bodrum Windmills

      The old windmills around Bodrum are counted among the special examples of Türkiye's traditional local architecture. Source: Wikipedia

    • Ölüdeniz Lagoon

      Ölüdeniz gets its name from its calm water, even during storms. The lagoon is a nature reserve where building is not allowed. Source: Wikipedia

    Antalya

    Route steps 85–96

    • Yivli Minaret and Kaleiçi

      The Yivli Minaret is a fluted brick minaret with traces of blue tiles. It has become a well-known symbol of Antalya. Source: Wikipedia

    • Hadrian's Gate

      Hadrian's Gate was built for Roman Emperor Hadrian, who visited Antalya in AD 130. Locals call it ‘Üçkapılar’, the Three Gates. Source: Wikipedia

    • Aspendos Theatre

      Aspendos has the best-preserved theatre of the ancient world. Opera and ballet festivals are still held there today. Source: Wikipedia

    • Düden Waterfalls

      At the Lower Düden Waterfall, the Düden River drops 40 metres straight into the Mediterranean Sea. Source: Wikipedia

    Konya

    Route steps 97–108

    • Mevlana Mausoleum

      The dome of the Mevlana Mausoleum is covered with turquoise tiles. It has been a museum since 1927. Source: Wikipedia

    • Alaeddin Mosque

      Alaeddin Mosque is the largest Seljuk mosque still standing in Konya. It is named after Sultan Alaaddin Keykubad I. Source: Wikipedia

    • Sille Village

      Sille has many small churches carved into soft volcanic rock. Its Aya Elena Church is believed to date back to AD 327. Source: Wikipedia

    • Çatalhöyük

      Çatalhöyük was settled around 7500 BC. Houses were built side by side, so rooftops worked like streets. It joined UNESCO's list in 2012. Source: Wikipedia

    Cappadocia

    Route steps 109–120

    • Fairy Chimneys

      Fairy chimneys formed over millions of years as wind and water wore away soft volcanic rock left by ancient volcanoes. Source: Wikipedia

    • Göreme Open-Air Museum

      Göreme has the most painted rock-cut churches in Cappadocia. Göreme National Park joined the UNESCO World Heritage List in 1985. Source: Wikipedia

    • Uçhisar Castle

      Uçhisar means ‘outer citadel’ and refers to its huge rock cone. In nearby Pigeon Valley, pigeon houses were carved into the rocks. Source: Wikipedia

    • Balloon Launch Field

      In Cappadocia, hot-air balloons take off at sunrise, floating over fairy chimneys and valleys, especially around Göreme. Source: Wikipedia

    Ankara

    Route steps 121–132

    • Anıtkabir

      Anıtkabir is the monument where Atatürk rests. Its construction began in 1944 and was completed in 1953. Source: Wikipedia

    • Ankara Castle

      The first fort on the site of Ankara Castle was built by the Phrygians in the 8th century BC. Romans, Byzantines, Seljuks and Ottomans later repaired it. Source: Wikipedia

    • Atakule Tower

      Atakule is 125 metres tall and opened in 1989. The restaurant at the top turns a full circle in one hour. Source: Wikipedia

    • Kocatepe Mosque

      Kocatepe Mosque is the largest mosque in Ankara. It was built from 1967 to 1987 and can be seen from much of the city. Source: Wikipedia

    Black Sea

    Route steps 133–144

    • Safranbolu Houses

      Safranbolu joined the UNESCO World Heritage list in 1994 thanks to its well-preserved Ottoman houses. Source: Wikipedia

    • Sumela Monastery

      Sumela Monastery is built into a steep cliff about 1,200 metres high. Its founding is dated to around AD 386. Source: Wikipedia

    • Lake Uzungöl

      Uzungöl means ‘Long Lake’. It formed when a landslide blocked a stream and made a natural dam. Source: Wikipedia

    • Rize Tea Gardens

      Tea farming in Rize began as an experiment in 1912. Its rainy climate is just right for growing tea. Source: Wikipedia

    Southeast Anatolia

    Route steps 145–156

    • Statues of Mount Nemrut

      In 62 BC, King Antiochus I built a tomb on the mountain top, guarded by giant statues 8 to 9 metres tall. Source: Wikipedia

    • Göbekli Tepe

      Its giant T-shaped stone pillars, from around 9500 BC, are among the oldest known megaliths. UNESCO listed it in 2018. Source: Wikipedia

    • Stone Houses of Mardin

      Mardin's old houses are built from local beige limestone. They step down the hillside, so the old town looks like a giant staircase. Source: Wikipedia

    • Halfeti

      When the Birecik Dam on the Euphrates filled in the 1990s, part of Halfeti ended up under the water. Source: Wikipedia

    Eastern Anatolia

    Route steps 157–168

    • Akdamar Church

      This church on an island in Lake Van was built from pink volcanic stone between 915 and 921. Source: Wikipedia

    • Ruins of Ani

      Ani was once nicknamed the ‘City of 1,001 Churches’, though the real number was smaller. It joined the UNESCO list in 2016. Source: Wikipedia

    • İshak Pasha Palace

      Work on the palace began in 1685 and went on for about 100 years, finishing around 1784. Source: Wikipedia

    • Mount Ararat

      At 5,137 metres, Mount Ararat is the highest peak in Türkiye. It is a snow-capped volcano that is fast asleep. Source: Wikipedia
