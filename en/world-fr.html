    France

    The France route: 3 regions, 12 landmarks. Every landmark is built in three stages.

    Paris

    Route steps 1–12

    • Eiffel Tower

      Built for the 1889 World's Fair, the tower is nicknamed the ‘Iron Lady’ because it is made of iron. Source: Wikipedia

    • Arc de Triomphe

      Beneath the arch lies the Tomb of the Unknown Soldier, honoured by a flame that never goes out. Source: Wikipedia

    • Notre-Dame

      Building the cathedral began in 1163 and was mostly finished by 1260, almost 100 years later. Source: Wikipedia

    • Sacré-Cœur and Montmartre

      The basilica's stone releases calcite when it rains, which keeps it bright white. Source: Wikipedia

    Normandy

    Route steps 13–24

    • Mont-Saint-Michel

      Long ago, pilgrims could walk to this island only at low tide. It became a UNESCO site in 1979. Source: Wikipedia

    • Étretat Cliffs

      Étretat's chalk cliffs have three natural arches and a pointed rock called the Needle, 70 metres above the sea. Source: Wikipedia

    • Honfleur Harbour

      Painters like Monet and Boudin often painted Honfleur's harbour houses, and their meetings helped Impressionism begin. Source: Wikipedia

    • Monet's Garden at Giverny

      The painter Monet spotted the village of Giverny from a train window and decided to move there. Source: Wikipedia

    Loire Valley

    Route steps 25–36

    • Château de Chambord

      At the château's centre is a double-spiral staircase: two stairways climb three floors without ever meeting. Source: Wikipedia

    • Château de Chenonceau

      Chenonceau stretches across the river Cher; its long gallery stands on a bridge over the water. Source: Wikipedia

    • Château d'Amboise

      Leonardo da Vinci came to Amboise in 1515 as the king's guest; his house was linked to the château by an underground passage. Source: Wikipedia

    • Gardens of Villandry

      In Villandry's Renaissance gardens, even the vegetables grow in neat patterns framed by low box hedges. Source: Wikipedia
