    Germany

    The Germany route: 3 regions, 12 landmarks. Every landmark is built in three stages.

    Hamburg

    Route steps 1–12

    • Elbphilharmonie

      This concert hall was built on top of an old brick warehouse and opened on 11 January 2017. Source: Wikipedia

    • Speicherstadt Warehouses

      Speicherstadt is the world's largest warehouse district standing on oak log foundations. It became a UNESCO site in 2015. Source: Wikipedia

    • Harbour and Cranes

      The Port of Hamburg is called Germany's ‘Gateway to the World’ and is the country's biggest seaport. Source: Wikipedia

    • St. Michael's Church

      The church's 132-metre copper-covered spire has long been a landmark guiding ships on the Elbe. Source: Wikipedia

    Berlin

    Route steps 13–24

    • Brandenburg Gate

      Built from 1788 to 1791, the gate is topped by a statue of a chariot pulled by four horses. Source: Wikipedia

    • Berlin TV Tower

      At 368 metres including its antenna, this tower is the tallest structure in Germany. Source: Wikipedia

    • Berlin Cathedral

      Today's cathedral was built on Museum Island from 1894 to 1905, ordered by Emperor William II. Source: Wikipedia

    • Oberbaum Bridge

      Opened in 1896, this double-deck bridge carries cars below and underground trains on top. Source: Wikipedia

    Cologne

    Route steps 25–36

    • Cologne Cathedral

      Work on the cathedral began in 1248 and it was only finished, to its original plan, in 1880. Source: Wikipedia

    • Hohenzollern Bridge

      Over 1,200 trains a day cross Germany's busiest railway bridge, and its fences are full of love padlocks. Source: Wikipedia

    • Colourful Old Town Houses

      The square in front of the colourful houses is called Fischmarkt, the fish market, a name first recorded in the 12th century. Source: Wikipedia

    • Rhine Cruise Boat

      About 1,230 km long, the Rhine has carried boats full of goods since Roman times. Source: Wikipedia
