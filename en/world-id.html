    Indonesia

    The Indonesia route: 3 regions, 12 landmarks. Every landmark is built in three stages.

    Jakarta

    Route steps 1–12

    • Monas (National Monument)

      This 132-metre monument is topped with a flame covered in gold leaf. Source: Wikipedia

    • Istiqlal Mosque

      Its name means ‘Independence Mosque’. Opened in 1978, it is the largest mosque in Southeast Asia. Source: Wikipedia

    • Jakarta Cathedral

      This neo-Gothic cathedral was consecrated in 1901, and Istiqlal Mosque stands right next to it. Source: Wikipedia

    • Kota Tua Old Town

      Kota Tua means ‘Old Town’; it was the first walled centre of Batavia, Jakarta's old name. Source: Wikipedia

    Yogyakarta

    Route steps 13–24

    • Borobudur

      This 9th-century Buddhist temple has 2,672 carved stone panels and originally had 504 Buddha statues. Source: Wikipedia

    • Prambanan

      Built in the 9th century, Prambanan is the largest Hindu temple site in Indonesia and a UNESCO World Heritage Site. Source: Wikipedia

    • Sultan's Palace (Kraton)

      Built in 1755–1756, the palace still hosts gamelan music, Javanese dance and shadow puppet shows. Source: Wikipedia

    • Mount Merapi

      Merapi means ‘Fire Mountain’, and it is the most active volcano in Indonesia. Source: Wikipedia

    Bromo · East Java

    Route steps 25–36

    • Mount Bromo

      Mount Bromo rises from the middle of a wide plain of volcanic sand called the ‘Sea of Sand’. Source: Wikipedia

    • Ijen Crater

      Ijen is famous for its glowing blue fire and a turquoise crater lake about one kilometre wide. Source: Wikipedia

    • Tumpak Sewu Waterfall

      Tumpak Sewu roughly means ‘a thousand waterfalls’ in Javanese, as many falls pour into one half-circle valley. Source: Wikipedia

    • Mount Semeru

      Semeru is the highest mountain on the island of Java. It is also called Mahameru, ‘the Great Mountain’. Source: Wikipedia
